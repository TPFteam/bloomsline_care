# Bloomsline

**A platform that supports people between therapy sessions — and gives practitioners visibility into what happens when their clients leave the room.**

We built a product before this one. It failed. But in 150+ conversations with practitioners and the people they support, we kept hearing the same thing: the hardest part of care isn't the session — it's everything after.

Bloomsline is what we built with that insight.

---

## The problem

Most people in therapy see their practitioner once a week. That's one hour. The rest of the week — 167 hours — they're on their own.

That's where the real work happens. And right now, nobody's helping with it.

**The person in care** has a hard day, forgets what they discussed, falls back into old patterns, and shows up next week starting over.

**The practitioner** asks *"how was your week?"*, gets a vague answer, and has to work with incomplete information.

It's not that therapy doesn't work. It's that nothing supports what happens in between.

**That's what Bloomsline does.**

---

## How we got here

In early 2023, Aditya and Sarah met with a shared frustration: finding a therapist felt broken. So they built **Doctolink** — a platform where practitioners could show who they really are. Not just degrees and reviews, but values, personality, how they actually work with people.

They talked to over 50 practitioners and 100 people looking for help.

Doctolink didn't work. The market was crowded, the timing was off, and there was no clear way to make it sustainable.

But something kept coming up in those conversations.

Practitioners didn't just want to be found. They wanted to *see* what happened to their clients between sessions. And clients didn't just want to find a therapist. They wanted to feel supported on Tuesday night — not just Thursday at 3pm.

The real problem wasn't matching. It was the silence between sessions.

That's where Bloomsline started.

> "We didn't build a product and then find a problem. We earned the insight through a product that failed."

## What Bloomsline actually is

Two simple ideas, connected.

### For the person in care

**Moments.** Capture a piece of your day — a photo, a voice note, a few words. Ten seconds. No streaks, no guilt if you skip a day.

Over time, these moments become a quiet record of your life. Not a journal you have to maintain. Just... evidence of showing up.

**Bloom.** An AI companion that's there when your practitioner isn't. Not therapy. Not advice. Just a thoughtful presence that helps you reflect, notices your patterns, and gently says *"Hey, on days you walk in the morning, you tend to feel calmer."*

### For the practitioner

**The full picture.** Instead of starting each session with *"So, how was your week?"*, they can see the journey between sessions. The good days. The hard ones. The patterns their client can't see themselves.

**Resources.** Share worksheets, exercises, reading — things that support progress between appointments. Not scattered across email and PDF. In one place, connected to the person.

**Less admin, more care.** Dashboard, scheduling, member management. But designed around understanding people, not just managing a business.

## What we believe

**Small things compound.** A photo of a morning walk doesn't feel like progress. But 30 of them tell a story about what makes someone feel alive.

**People don't need more apps.** They need something that fits into their life without asking for much. Ten seconds, not ten minutes.

**Care shouldn't stop at the door.** The most important moments happen outside the therapy room. That's where Bloomsline lives.

**Practitioners are the bridge.** The B2C wellness app graveyard is full of good ideas that people download, use twice, and delete. When a practitioner says *"try this"*, people actually do it. That relationship is everything.

**AI should support, not replace.** Bloom isn't a therapist. It's a companion. It helps people notice things about themselves. It's there at 2am when no one else is. But it always knows when to say *"talk to your practitioner about this."*

## The opportunity

The digital mental health market is going from $7.5B to $47B by 2035.

But here's what matters more than market size:

- **68%** of practices now use tele-mental-health
- Medicare started covering digital mental health in 2025
- Practice management tools (SimplePractice, Jane) handle billing and scheduling — but none of them help with what happens between sessions
- Wellness apps (Headspace, Calm) are generic — high download, high churn, no practitioner connection

**The gap between "managing a practice" and "delivering care" is wide open. That's where we sit.**

Our model is B2B2C. We sell to practitioners. They bring their members. One practitioner = 20-50 members. Low acquisition cost, high retention (because the practitioner relationship keeps people engaged), natural word-of-mouth growth.

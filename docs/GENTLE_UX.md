# Bloomsline — Gentle UX (GUX) Design Philosophy

## What is Gentle UX?

Gentle UX is Bloomsline's design standard for emotional contexts. It's not just a design system — it's a philosophy that directly drives retention.

> Most mental health apps copy fitness patterns: streaks, scores, guilt. 90% of users quit within a month. Gentle UX is our answer.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Progress as Narrative, Not Numbers
- No streak counters, no scores, no leaderboards
- Progress is shown through stories: "You've been reflecting more this week" instead of "7-day streak!"
- Growth is framed as a journey, not a competition

### 2. No Pressure Mechanics
- No guilt for missing a day
- No push notifications that shame ("You haven't logged in 3 days!")
- No loss aversion tactics (losing streaks, dropping levels)
- The app welcomes you back warmly, no matter how long you've been away

### 3. The Interface Itself is Therapeutic
- Soft colors, gentle transitions, breathing space
- Micro-interactions that feel calming, not gamified
- Typography that's warm and readable, not clinical
- Animations that flow naturally (Framer Motion for web, React Native Animated for mobile)

### 4. Bilingual & Culturally Sensitive
- Full EN/FR support throughout
- Language toggle that preserves context
- Cultural sensitivity in metaphors and imagery

---

## GUX vs. Traditional App Design

| Traditional Apps | Gentle UX |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Streaks (7-day, 30-day) | Narrative progress ("growing steadily") |
| Scores and points | Qualitative insights |
| Red notifications for inactivity | Warm welcome-back messages |
| Gamification (badges, levels) | Natural growth metaphors |
| Competitive leaderboards | Personal journey focus |
| Guilt-driven retention | Trust-driven retention |

---

## Why GUX = Retention

The mental health app space has a massive retention problem:
- **90% of users quit within a month** (typical wellness apps)
- The apps that copy fitness patterns (streaks, scores) inadvertently create anxiety in users already dealing with mental health challenges
- Guilt-based retention works for fitness apps but is counterproductive for therapeutic contexts

GUX flips this:
- Users don't feel punished for stepping away → they come back
- Progress feels personal and meaningful → they engage deeper
- The experience feels safe → they share more → the platform becomes more valuable

> "That's not design. That's retention."

## GUX in Practice (Examples)

### Seeds/Anchors Screen (Mobile App)
- Daily habits shown as "seeds" that grow — botanical metaphor instead of checkboxes
- Missing a day doesn't break a streak — the seed is still growing
- Week view shows dots, not scores
- Month view uses gentle color gradients, not harsh red/green

### Moments Capture
- "Capture a moment" — not "Log your mood"
- 10-second interaction — photo, voice, or text
- No forced categorization — AI discovers patterns naturally
- Emphasis on self-expression, not data entry

### Practitioner Dashboard
- Member engagement shown as narrative summaries
- No alarming red flags — gentle attention indicators
- Respectful of the therapeutic relationship

## The Competitive Moat

> Competitors can copy features. They can't copy a philosophy baked into every interaction.

GUX isn't a feature that can be added with a sprint. It requires:
- Deep understanding of therapeutic contexts
- Intentional design decisions at every level
- Willingness to sacrifice "engagement metrics" for genuine wellbeing
- Cultural sensitivity built from the ground up

This makes it a genuine competitive moat — not because it's technically hard, but because it requires a fundamentally different mindset than most tech companies operate with.

## Research Foundations

GUX draws from established movements in design:
- **Calm Technology** (Amber Case) — technology should require the minimum amount of attention
- **Compassionate Design** — design that acknowledges human vulnerability
- **Trauma-Informed Design** — design that avoids re-traumatizing users
- **Restorative UX** — design that actively contributes to wellbeing

Bloomsline's contribution is applying these principles specifically to the between-session care context and codifying them as **Gentle UX** — a design standard for emotional contexts.

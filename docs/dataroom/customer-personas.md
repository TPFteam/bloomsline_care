## Marie — Independent Psychotherapist (P0)

**Quote:** "I became a therapist to help people heal, not to spend my Sundays doing paperwork."

### Demographics
- Age: 35-45
- Income: €45-65K/yr
- Education: Master's in Psychology
- Location: Paris / Lyon
- Job: Psychotherapist (solo)
- Family: Partner, 0-2 children

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Client wellbeing above all, Evidence-based practice, Work-life balance, Continuous learning
- **Beliefs:** Therapy works when sustained, Admin kills care quality, Tech should be invisible, EU tools should exist
- **Personality:** Empathetic & detail-oriented, Skeptical of "shiny" tech, Pragmatic problem-solver, Values peer validation

### Pain Points
1. 5-8 hrs/week on admin (notes, scheduling, follow-ups)
2. "167-hour blind spot" — zero visibility between sessions
3. Clients drop off silently with no early warning
4. No EU-native, affordable tools — forced to use US software or spreadsheets
5. Can't prove outcomes to referral partners or insurance

### Goals
1. Spend 80%+ of work time on actual therapy
2. See clients progressing between sessions
3. Reduce no-shows and silent dropoffs
4. Have one tool for notes, scheduling, and client engagement

### Buying Behavior
- **Discovery:** LinkedIn peer recommendations, AFTCC/FFPP conference demos, Word-of-mouth from training cohort
- **Evaluation:** Free trial (must work in <10 min), Checks RGPD compliance first, Compares to current workflow cost
- **Purchase:** Monthly subscription (no annual lock-in), Solo decision — no committee, Converts within 2-4 weeks of trial

### Objections
1. "I don't have time to learn another tool" — Needs onboarding in <10 minutes
2. "Is my client data really safe?" — RGPD/GDPR compliance is non-negotiable
3. "My clients aren't tech-savvy enough" — Needs proof member app works for all ages

### Trigger Events
- Loses a client who "just stopped coming" → Guilt + frustration
- Sunday evening spent catching up on notes → Resentment
- Referral partner asks for outcome data → Embarrassment

### Willingness to Pay
- Range: €19-49/mo
- 1 cancelled session = €60-80 lost; Current US tools cost €50-139/mo
- Sensitivity: Medium — price-conscious but values time savings

## Thomas — Group Practice Director (P1)

**Quote:** "I manage 6 practitioners and I have no idea how their clients are actually doing."

### Demographics
- Age: 40-55
- Income: €65-90K/yr
- Education: Master's + management training
- Location: Metro France (Paris, Bordeaux, Marseille)
- Job: Practice Director / Clinical Lead
- Family: Partner, 1-3 children

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Team wellbeing & retention, Operational efficiency, Evidence-based outcomes
- **Beliefs:** Good tools reduce burnout, Data drives better care, Standardization enables quality
- **Personality:** Analytical & systems-oriented, Risk-averse with new vendors, ROI-driven

### Pain Points
1. No unified view across 3-8 practitioners — each uses different tools
2. Can't prove outcomes to referral partners, insurers, or funders
3. Staff burn out on admin — loses 1-2 practitioners/year
4. Onboarding new practitioners takes weeks of manual setup
5. No way to identify at-risk clients across the practice

### Goals
1. Single platform for entire team with unified client records
2. Prove clinical outcomes to referral partners and insurers
3. Reduce admin load to improve practitioner retention
4. Scalable system that grows with the practice

### Buying Behavior
- **Discovery:** Team member recommends after personal trial, Industry conference, Peer practice director referral
- **Evaluation:** Pilot with 1-2 practitioners (4-6 weeks), ROI analysis, Security audit + RGPD verification
- **Purchase:** Per-practitioner pricing (volume), Annual contract preferred, 6-12 week decision cycle

### Objections
1. "My team won't adopt another tool" — Needs proof of minimal learning curve
2. "I need ROI data to justify the expense" — Requires cost-benefit analysis
3. "What about data migration?" — Needs import tools or white-glove onboarding

### Willingness to Pay
- Range: €20-25/practitioner/mo
- Losing 1 practitioner = €50-80K replacement cost
- Sensitivity: Low — volume buyer, needs ROI justification not lowest price

## Lea — Engaged Young Professional (P1, B2C)

**Quote:** "My therapist gives me amazing insights during sessions, but by Wednesday I can't remember half of what we talked about."

### Demographics
- Age: 26-34
- Income: €35-55K/yr
- Education: Bachelor's / Master's
- Location: Paris (urban)
- Job: Marketing / Tech / Consulting
- Family: Single or partner, no kids

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Personal growth & self-awareness, Mental health destigmatization, Digital-first solutions
- **Beliefs:** Therapy is an investment not a weakness, Progress should be visible, Good UX matters for health tools
- **Personality:** Growth-oriented & curious, Willing to try new apps, Abandons apps with bad UX quickly

### Pain Points
1. Forgets therapy insights by day 3
2. Journaling apps feel generic — not connected to actual care plan
3. No way to share progress moments with therapist between sessions
4. Meditation apps (Headspace, Calm) feel disconnected from real therapy
5. Wants to track progress but doesn't know what to measure

### Buying Behavior
- **Discovery:** 100% via therapist recommendation — never finds it alone
- **Evaluation:** Tries free version immediately, Compares to Headspace/Calm, Needs value within first week
- **Purchase:** Free via practitioner initially, Projected 5-8% convert to ~€3/mo premium (hypothesis)

### Willingness to Pay
- Range: €0 to start, ~€3/mo premium
- Headspace: €12.99/mo, Calm: €11.99/mo — Bloomsline member is much cheaper
- Sensitivity: High — expects free core, pays for extras

## Sophie — Overwhelmed Parent (P2, B2C)

**Quote:** "I know therapy is helping, but by the time I get the kids to bed, I can barely remember what my therapist said."

### Demographics
- Age: 38-48
- Income: €22-35K/yr
- Education: Bac+2 / BTS
- Location: Suburban France
- Job: Part-time (admin / retail / care)
- Family: Partner + 2 children

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Family stability above all, Practical over theoretical, Trust in her therapist
- **Beliefs:** Tech is for younger people, Free tools have hidden costs, She shouldn't need help with help
- **Personality:** Warm but exhausted, Low tech confidence, Needs encouragement not instructions

### Pain Points
1. Zero time — mornings are chaos, evenings are exhaustion
2. Feels guilty about therapy cost — needs to feel it's "working"
3. Forgets homework assignments and feels ashamed at next session
4. Apps feel intimidating — "too many buttons, not sure what to do"
5. Doesn't want to "bother" her therapist between sessions

### Buying Behavior
- **Discovery:** 100% via therapist — sets it up IN session on her phone
- **Evaluation:** No evaluation phase — if therapist sets it up, she uses it
- **Purchase:** €0 forever — by design. Her engagement IS the product value (proves B2B outcomes)

### Retention Risk
If first use takes >30 seconds to understand, she never opens it again. Onboarding must happen IN session with practitioner guiding. No self-serve setup. No tutorial screens. Open → one action → done.
